Fate points

Fate points work as in Fate Core, with three Hunt rules: refresh per mission, trait cards and rival compels.

Refresh: every mission

Each hunter has a refresh of 3 (one free stunt). At the start of each mission, the GM runs the Start of Mission macro: every hunter owned by a player goes back to their refresh. Points above the refresh are kept. Points are not refreshed between sessions of the same mission.

Spending a Fate point

  • Invoke an aspect (yours, a zone's, a target's, a consequence): +2 to your total, or reroll your four dice. Several aspects can be invoked on one roll, each once.
  • Play an active trait (red band): play the card to Played Traits, the point is taken from your assigned character automatically. Triggered traits cost nothing.
  • Compel a rival: see below.
  • Refuse a compel.

Free invokes (from creating an advantage, from a stunt such as Pathfinder, from tools such as Decoys or the Stalker Beetle) cost no Fate point.

The Fate point button

Every single-target attack card has a Fate point button. The attacker's owner can invoke for the attack, the target's owner (if the target was aware) for the defence:

  1. Choose who invokes, name the aspect, pick +2 or reroll. Tick Free invoke if it doesn't cost a point.
  2. The point is taken from the sheet, and a new card shows the new totals and result (a new ballistic die if it becomes a hit), with its own Apply button.
  3. The old card is greyed out and its Apply button is disabled. Both sides can keep invoking on the newest card.

Ammunition and weapon stress are not spent again. For area, cone and blast attacks, add the +2 in the attack dialog's Other bonus and take the point off the sheet by hand.

Earning Fate points

  • Accept a compel, from the GM or from a rival: +1.
  • Someone invokes one of your aspects against you (a hostile invoke): you earn that point at the end of the scene.
  • Concede a conflict before the roll: +1, plus 1 per consequence you took in it. In Hunt, conceding means running, hiding or leaving the Bounty behind — the GM and the winner say what you lose.

Rival compels

Hunt is played team against team. A hunter can compel a rival hunter's aspect, but only in the same compound:

  1. The compeller pays 1 Fate point and says how the rival's aspect gets them in trouble now (Trigger-Happy: they fire and give away their position).
  2. The rival accepts: they take that Fate point and play the complication. Or they refuse: they pay 1 Fate point themselves, and nothing happens.

The GM confirms the compel is fair. Take the points on the sheets by hand.

Monsters

Bosses have 2 Fate points for the encounter; Grunts and Elites have none. The GM does not keep a separate pool.
